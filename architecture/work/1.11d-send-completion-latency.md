# 1.11d: Why netvsc's send completions take 10–26 ms on Azure

- **System design:** `architecture/moruna-os-system.md`, section 1F (Azure, VMBus, netvsc). **Rules:** `architecture/work/_rules.md`, and 6.3's steps: diagnose, then stop and report before fixing.

## The lead

In 1.11c's traces (`~/moruna-data/azure/console-trace-1.log` and `console-trace-2.log`):
- send completions arrive 10–26 ms after the commit;
- the on-host round trips are 11 ms (ARP to the gateway) and 13 ms (DISCOVER to OFFER);
- completions often arrive in the same instant as the next received frame (for example 4948.353 and 4948.359 ms).

Linux's hv_netvsc on the same host is generally reported well under a millisecond for these. Either the host batches completions and signals late, or netvsc's wake path (the VMBus channel's interrupt or signal, the vmbus process, or netvsc's own wait) is slow. This bounds every request and response the node makes, including 3.4's object reads.

## Do

1. **Read** netvsc's send and completion path and the VMBus signalling from host to guest:
   - the interrupt mask and pending-send-size;
   - batched signalling;
   - the wake channel;
   - how the vmbus process and netvsc wait.

   Compare it with Linux's hv_netvsc and hv_vmbus: `vmbus_setevent`, `hv_signal_on_read`, `pending_send_sz` and the NAPI poll.
2. **Name the likely cause** in our code, or say why it is the host's. Write a host test where the library can show it.
3. **Write a light trace** that would prove it on Azure: timestamps for the commit, the host's interrupt reaching vmbus, the wake of netvsc and the completion read. Put it behind the existing `trace-first-frames` feature, and give the PM the image command.
4. **Stop and report** before fixing.

## Report

The reading, the hypotheses ranked, any test, and the image command.
