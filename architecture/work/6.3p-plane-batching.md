# 6.3p: the ring plane loses its batching when the task is woken quickly

- **System design:** the plane (OS design section 4, "The plane"; `kernel/sys/src/plane.rs`, the
  ring protocol between the engine and a task). **Rules:** `architecture/work/_rules.md`, and 6.3's
  steps.
- **Found by:** 1.11e's boots (2026-11-18). The boot test DS-P steady (the engine's and the task's
  calls per batch in the steady state; it fails at 1.0) fails as follows:
  - at 4 CPUs, on the old scheduler and the new one alike (1.10–1.20 calls a batch in failing
    boots);
  - at 1 CPU, on every boot under 1.11e's rule (1.08–1.14), against 0.34–0.39 on the old one.

## The defect

The plane batches only by accident of timing. A task that finishes a batch spins `SPINS` times
(256 reads, "a few microseconds", `plane.rs` about line 619) and sleeps. The engine's next post then
costs a wake. When the task is woken promptly (1.11e's fix, or a free CPU at 4 CPUs), it takes one
batch, sleeps, and is woken again. The old scheduler kept the plane batching only because it made
the task wait behind other threads for up to a 10 ms slice.

The plane's efficiency should not depend on the scheduler being slow.

## Do

1. **Write the defect as a test:** on the host model of the plane if one exists, else a booted
   check. With a task that is woken at once, the steady state stays under one call a batch.
2. **Propose, then build,** a plane rule that batches by design: for example, the engine rings the
   task's bell once per group of posts rather than per post; or the task waits a measured, bounded
   time for more work before sleeping (an adaptive spin set from the posting rate). Say what each
   costs in latency for a single batch, and choose by measurement. Ask the PM before changing the
   ring protocol's format.
3. **Show DS-P steady passing** at 1 and 4 CPUs on 1.11e's scheduler, and KK-T6 and the plane's
   latency tests unchanged.

## Report

The test, the rule chosen with its measurements, the booted lines, coverage and the commits.
